What a diagnostic check includes

A diagnostic check finds out what is actually wrong before you spend money on a repair. Here is what it covers, what it can’t promise, and how you hear the result.

What a check covers

Which of these apply depends on your device and the fault.

  • Symptom review. What you see, when it started and what happened before.
  • Hardware checks. Basic checks of the parts that could cause the symptom.
  • Software and settings checks. Updates, drivers, start-up items and malware where relevant.
  • Storage, battery and power checks. Condition of the drive, battery and charging path.
  • A clear explanation. The likely cause, in words that make sense.
  • Options and an estimate. What can be done, what it would cost, and what is not worth doing.

What a check can’t promise

  • That every fault can be repaired, or repaired at a sensible cost.
  • That lost files can be recovered. That depends on the condition of the storage.
  • A final repair price before the fault is known. Starting prices are a guide.
  • That the online request builder diagnoses anything. It only builds a WhatsApp message.

How you hear the result

You get the findings on WhatsApp in plain language, in a format like these examples. You decide what happens next, and paid repair work starts only after you agree.

Diagnostic report: Slow laptop

Example format, not a real customer record
Device
Laptop (example)
Fault described
Takes several minutes to start and freezes when more than a couple of programs are open.
Likely cause
The drive is almost full and many programs start with Windows. An older hard drive may also be the bottleneck.
Recommended action
Clean up start-up programs and files first. If the drive is the bottleneck, consider an SSD upgrade.
Estimate
Check from R80. Any cleanup or upgrade is confirmed after the check, and an SSD is quoted separately.
Honest risk note
Reinstalling Windows can erase files, so backups come first.
After the work
Start-up time and responsiveness are checked again after the work.

Diagnostic report: Phone not charging

Example format, not a real customer record
Device
Phone (example)
Fault described
Only charges when the cable is held at an angle, or not at all.
Likely cause
Often a worn cable, lint in the port or a worn port. Less often the battery.
Recommended action
Try a known-good cable and charger, clean the port, then test the battery.
Estimate
Phone check-up from R80. Any part is quoted separately before work.
Honest risk note
Port and board repairs carry risk and may not be possible. I will say so before starting.
After the work
Charging speed and stability are checked after the fix.

Diagnostic report: Black screen

Example format, not a real customer record
Device
Laptop (example)
Fault described
Powers on and the fan spins, but the screen stays black.
Likely cause
Could be the screen, its cable, the memory or a deeper fault. Testing with an external monitor helps narrow it down.
Recommended action
Test with an external display, reseat the memory and inspect the connections, then decide on a repair.
Estimate
Check from R80. A repair is only quoted once the fault is known.
Honest risk note
If the fault is on the motherboard, repair may not be worth the cost. I will tell you honestly.
After the work
Display and start-up are checked after any repair.

These examples are written to show the format. They are not real customer reports, and the estimates only restate the starting prices.
